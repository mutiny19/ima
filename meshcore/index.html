Get on the Indiana mesh

From a new radio to your first message in five steps. You'll hear everything the repeaters around you pass along, with nothing to set up for regions.

Default: openHear everythingNo region setup needed
Indiana's starting point: nothing to scope. Leave every message and channel unscoped and you reach the most contacts, including across state lines. If a channel gets too busy, mute or leave it rather than scoping it. Repeater owners have their own page: Indiana region settings.

First radio to first message

Get a radio

Any device the MeshCore flasher lists works; a pocket tracker with a built-in battery is the easiest start. Buy the US 915 MHz version. More in the FAQ: what you need to start.

Install Companion firmware

Open the MeshCore flasher in Chrome or Edge on a computer, plug the radio in by USB, pick your device and choose the Companion (Bluetooth) firmware.

Install the app and pair

Get the MeshCore app for iPhone, Android or the web. Connect to your radio over Bluetooth and give yourself a name.

Set the US radio settings

In the radio settings pick the USA preset. It must match these, or you'll hear nothing:

Frequency910.525 MHz
Bandwidth62.5 kHz
SpreadingSF7
Coding rateCR5

New radios often ship on the European 869 MHz setting. Always check this step.

Why these numbers: supported frequencies in the MeshCore FAQ.

Say hello

  • Send a flood advert so nearby people and repeaters learn about you.
  • Post in Public, or join #test and send a test message.
  • Seeing "heard repeats" after you send means a repeater picked you up.

Heard nothing? Recheck the radio settings in step 4, move the radio higher, then ask in the Indiana Mesh Discord where the nearest repeater is.

Hear everything around you

Leave scope off

Don't set a region or default scope in the app. Unscoped messages go out to everyone, and you still receive everything that reaches you.

Join the channels people use

Public is there from the start. Add hashtag channels like #test, #hamradio or #wardriving. In #porchlight, people share the names of channels worth joining.

Get some height

A window or a higher shelf helps more than anything else. Your radio only needs to reach one repeater; the repeaters do the rest.

Near a state line?

Nothing extra to set. Ohio, Kentucky, Michigan and Illinois use the same radio settings as Indiana, so you hear repeaters on both sides.

  • Keep scope off, so people across the line hear you and their replies reach you.
  • Join the group on the other side too: their channels and people are part of your local mesh.
  • A scoped message stops at the first repeater without its tag. Unscoped is what crosses borders.

Find people near you

Start with Indiana Mesh, the statewide community on Discord: ask where the closest repeater is and who's on the air. Near a state line, the neighbouring group is worth joining too.

Set it up

Firmware 1.16 and newer

region def us us-in|us us-oh|us us-ky|us us-mi|us us-il
region allowf *
region save
region list allowed

|us means “go back to us”, so every state lands under us. Check the version first with ver. Full command reference: Region Management.

Firmware 1.15 and openHop

region put us
region put us-in us
region put us-oh us
region put us-ky us
region put us-mi us
region put us-il us
region allowf *
region save

One region per line. Paste the lines one at a time.

  1. Run the commands on the repeater: USB serial, or the app's remote admin.
  2. region list allowed should show * plus all six names.
  3. Companions need nothing.

Keep the mesh open

Default open. Repeaters carry region names so other groups' scoped traffic passes through Indiana. Adding a region never blocks anything; each name only lets more scoped traffic through. Only region default, region denyf * and flood.max.unscoped restrict traffic, and Phase 1 leaves all three off.

Do

  • Keep unscoped traffic on with region allowf *.
  • Type names exactly: lowercase, no #. us-in is not in or US-IN.
  • Leave every message and channel unscoped. Mute a busy channel instead of scoping it.

Don't

  • Set region default on repeaters or companions.
  • Run region denyf * or set flood.max.unscoped.
  • Scope messages or channels as a new user. It limits who hears you.
  • Put comments in commands. Every extra word becomes a region.
In depth: how scoping works
  • Unscoped is the default and stays on.A message with no scope is relayed by every repeater that allows *, which is all of them in Phase 1.
  • A scoped message needs its exact tag on every repeater along the way.A repeater without that tag silently drops it. One repeater with no regions at all drops every scoped message. sends ind → Indiana repeater (us, us-in, us-oh, us-ky, us-mi, us-il) → dropped, no ind tag sends us-in → same repeater → forwarded
  • Matching is per tag, never by hierarchy.us does not cover us-in. A border repeater carries several states; a message scoped us-in stops at the first repeater that lacks it. sends us → every repeater carries us → crosses all states
  • Scope controls what you send, not what you hear.Any radio that hears a packet can read it, channel keys aside. A companion just over a state line still hears a message its nearby repeater relayed.
  • Watch for the one-way reply trap.If someone replies scoped to a tag your side's repeaters lack, you never see it. Leaving messages unscoped avoids this entirely.
  • Defaults change your own traffic too.A repeater or companion with a default region sends its own adverts and messages scoped. Phase 1 sets no default, so everything you send reaches everyone.
